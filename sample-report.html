3. Findings and business consequences

F-01 · HIGH PRIORITY · SAMPLE OBSERVATION

Email authentication enforcement review

Observation: Hypothetical DMARC policy configured for monitoring without enforcement. This does not itself prove successful impersonation.

Why it matters: An organization with weak or incomplete authenticated-mail alignment may find it harder to distinguish legitimate mail from spoofed attempts, increasing business-email fraud exposure.

Recommended next step: Email administrator inventories legitimate senders, checks SPF/DKIM alignment, evaluates reports, then phases in an appropriate DMARC enforcement policy.

Owner: IT/email provider · Effort: Needs scoping · Recheck: Confirm updated DNS policy and alignment following change.

Evidence classification: Fictional public DNS observation · Confidence: Not applicable to a real system.

F-02 · MODERATE · SAMPLE OBSERVATION

Response-header hardening

Observation: Illustrative website response omits a protective browser header.

Business relevance: Browser controls can reduce exposure to certain classes of web attacks but are not proof that an application is otherwise vulnerable.

Recommended next step: Website maintainer validates a compatible header configuration in staging and retests public responses.

Owner: Web administrator · Recheck: HTTP response header inspection.

U-01 · NOT VERIFIED · NOT A FAILED CONTROL

Privileged administrator MFA

Observation: Not accessible within public-domain assessment scope.

Recommended next step: If a tenant review is commissioned, collect the narrowly authorized read-only configuration evidence. Until then, do not mark MFA as enabled or disabled.

5. Delivery and acceptance

Final customer packages are intended to include a signed scope reference, assessment timestamp, source/evidence register, findings with confidence and limitations, a prioritized action table, review notes, and an agreed handoff discussion. Actual reports must never carry fabricated observations.
